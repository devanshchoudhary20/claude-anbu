# 11 · Behavioral

Panel: every senior loop. FAANG has a dedicated round and a bar-raiser; startups fold it into the founder chat; India product companies run it as the hiring manager round. Senior candidates fail here more than on code, because they tell project stories, not ownership stories. Interleaved from week 3: one story a week.

Ledger items: `story-ownership`, `story-conflict`, `story-failure`, `story-influence`, `story-ambiguity`, `story-mentoring`, `story-tradeoff`, `story-shipped-under-pressure`, `why-this-company`, `questions-for-them`.

## The shape

STAR with numbers: Situation in one sentence, Task as what you owned, Action as what you did (first person, specific, with the decision you made and the option you rejected), Result with a number and what you would change. Ninety seconds spoken. Every story must survive "what did you personally do" three times.

## Probe

1. Tell me about a project you drove end to end. Then: what did you decide that someone else would have decided differently.
2. A disagreement with a backend engineer or a PM. How it ended. What you would do differently.
3. A production incident you caused or owned. Timeline, what you did at each step, what changed afterward.
4. A time you changed the team's direction without authority.
5. Ambiguous requirements. What you did before writing code.
6. Someone junior you helped. What specifically changed in their work.
7. A tradeoff you made under deadline that you still think was right. One you regret.
8. Why this company. Why leave. What do you want in two years.

## Build

1. Draft eight stories in `design/stories.md`, one per item above, STAR with a number in each. Pull from LambdaTest work: KaneAI features, the contract tooling, the shared-package work, the review process [8 x 30 min, one a week].
2. Rehearse each aloud to the sensei, who plays the bar-raiser: three "what did you personally do" probes, then a score.
3. Write ten questions to ask interviewers that reveal how the team actually works, in the same file.

## Follow-ups

- "You said 'we'. Who."
- "What was the number before and after."
- "If your manager told this story, what would differ."
- "What is the smallest thing you would change in that story if you did it again."

## Pass bar

Eight stories drafted, each rehearsed at score 4 or above with no "we" left, each under two minutes.
